Your client used to search on Google, open five tabs and compare. Today they type "recommend a [what you sell] in [city]" and get three names. If yours isn't there, you didn't lose a click: you lost the whole conversation.

And almost nobody clicks the sources. In a Pew Research Center study (July 2025, 900 adults), only 1% of people clicked a link inside Google's AI summary. Everyone else decides by reading the answer.

This guide is written for sales teams, not developers. If you only have 15 minutes, run the test in chapter 2: it tells you which chapter comes next. An honest heads-up: almost every study on this topic is published by companies that sell SEO tools, and they show correlation, not causation.

## 1. How AI decides who to recommend

When someone asks for "the best [category] for [use case]," ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode almost always search the web right then. They don't answer from memory: they split the question into several smaller searches, read lists, reviews, forums, videos and websites, and name the brands that show up again and again.

<!-- anim: chatgpt-recomiende-tu-marca/consenso-de-fuentes | The AI splits one question into several searches, reads lists, reviews, forums and videos, and names the brands the most sources repeat, not the one with the best website -->

**The brand most people mention wins, not the one with the best website.** AI rewards consensus. If lots of independent sources talk about you, it names you. If only your own site talks about you, it almost never does. In an Ahrefs analysis of 75,000 brands (December 2025), brand mentions across the web were linked to showing up in AI answers far more than domain authority, and YouTube mentions were the strongest signal.

**There's no such thing as "number 1 on ChatGPT."** The same question returns different lists almost every time. What you measure is how many answers you show up in, not what position you're in.

**Do it today:** write down the 5 questions your ideal client would ask an AI before buying from you.

## 2. The 15-minute test

Before you change anything, see where you stand. Open a new chat in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Paste each prompt, swap what's in [brackets] and run it 3 times, because the answers change.

<!-- anim: chatgpt-recomiende-tu-marca/apareces-en-x-de-3 | The same prompt run 3 times in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity returns different lists; what you count is how many answers your brand shows up in -->

1. **Does it recommend you?** "I'm a [type of buyer] in [city or state] looking for [what you sell]. What are the 5 best options and why? Cite your sources."
2. **Who are you losing to?** "Compare [your brand] with [competitor A] and [competitor B] for [use case]. Who is each one best for? Tell me which sources you used."
3. **What do your clients say?** "What do real customers say about [your brand]? Sum up the good, the bad and the most common complaints, and tell me where those opinions come from."
4. **Does it know you well?** "What is [your brand]? What it does, who it's for and where it's located."
5. **Where do you need to show up?** "Which websites, Reddit threads, reviews or YouTube videos would you read to decide the best [category] for [use case]? Give me the exact URLs."

Log it in a sheet with today's date: whether you show up (in how many of the 3 runs and in which AI), who shows up instead of you, which URLs repeat, whether it says anything false about you and the exact sentence it uses to describe you.

## 3. SEO and GEO: what actually matters

SEO is showing up in Google's results. GEO (generative engine optimization) is showing up inside AI answers. In May 2026 Google published its official guide and was blunt: its AI features don't need anything special. Optimizing for AI is doing good SEO, with original, useful content you can't find the same way anywhere else. If search engines can't see you, neither can AI.

**The basics you need to have:**

- One page per service, with the service and the city in the title.
- The important stuff in text, not images: what you do, who it's for, where and a price range.
- Real FAQs, the ones you get in sales, answered in two or three sentences.
- A complete Google Business Profile if you sell in a local area.
- Key pages kept current with real information. Changing the date without changing anything doesn't count.

**Send this to whoever runs your website** and ask for a yes or no:

```
1. Do our robots.txt and firewall let in OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Bingbot and Googlebot?
2. Is the site verified in Google Search Console and in Bing Webmaster Tools?
3. Is anything important (services, prices, city) only visible in images or behind heavy JavaScript?
```

The first one matters because OpenAI says in its documentation that if you block OAI-SearchBot, your site won't show up in ChatGPT's search answers.

**Myths not to buy into:** that you need an llms.txt file (Google says it doesn't use it), that you need special schema for AI, that someone can put you "at number 1 on ChatGPT" or that backlinks are the only thing that matters.

## 4. Reddit and forums

Reddit is one of the domains AI cites the most, especially Perplexity, but its weight goes up and down month to month. Treat it as one more channel, not your only bet. The upside: cited threads tend to be months or years old, so a good answer today keeps working for a long time.

How to join in without getting banned:

1. Read each community's rules. Moderators are stricter than the platform.
2. Say where you work. Hiding it is what gets people banned the most.
3. Answer fully before you mention your product. Most of your answers shouldn't name it at all.
4. Never buy upvotes or use fake accounts.
5. Start with the threads AI already cites (prompt 5 gives them to you).

```
I work at [your brand], so keep that in mind. For what you're describing, what matters most is [criterion 1] and [criterion 2]. If your budget is under [amount], [competitor] might be a better fit. We're a good fit when [specific situation].
```

Naming a competitor and saying who you're not for earns the community's trust, and gets AI to cite you.

## 5. Reviews, lists and YouTube

This is where most of the work is: getting other people to talk about you where AI reads.

**Reviews, the easiest thing to move.** When the buyer is about to decide, review sites carry much more weight in what AI cites. B2B software: G2 and Capterra. Consumers: Trustpilot and Google. Local businesses: Google Business Profile and Yelp.

```
Hi [name], I'm glad [specific result they got]. Could you help me out with a short review here? [link]. It takes 2 minutes and helps others like you find us.
```

**"Best of" lists.** They're some of the pages ChatGPT cites most when it recommends. Use prompt 5 to see which lists it cites in your category and email the author a one-page summary, pricing and 2 real cases. If you publish your own comparison, make it honest and say who you're not for.

**YouTube.** Record a short demo with your brand name in the title, ask 2 or 3 creators in your niche for a tutorial and turn every success story into a video.

**One description everywhere.** If your site says one thing, LinkedIn another and your Google listing another, AI has nothing clear to repeat:

```
[Your brand] helps [type of client] in [area] [result] with [how you do it]. We mostly work with [industry or size]. Plans range from [price] to [price].
```

## 6. Measure and repeat

If almost nobody clicks the sources, your analytics see very little: the client reads the answer, searches your name and arrives "direct." That's why you measure three ways: ask every lead how they found you, look in Google Analytics for visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and gemini.google.com (treat it as the floor, not the total) and rerun the chapter 2 test every month with the same prompts.

| Week | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Chapter 2 test, message to your web team, "how did you find us?" in your forms |
| 2 | Active review profile, 10 review requests, your brand paragraph everywhere |
| 3 | 3 answers on Reddit or forums, email to 5 list authors |
| 4 | An honest comparison, a video with your brand in the title, rerun the test |

## Do it with dForms

Two forms cover measurement and reviews, the two levers that depend on you the most.

**1. "How did you find us?" in your lead form.** Open the form that already gets your leads (or create one with **Forms → Create form** and the **Lead qualifier** template) and add two questions before the contact details:

- How did you find us? (single choice: ChatGPT or another AI, Google, Social media, Someone referred me, Other)
- What did you ask the AI or Google before you got here? (short text, optional)

Connect HubSpot in **Connect → Integrations** and map the first one to a "Self-reported source" property. In a month you'll know how many leads AI sends you, and the answers to the second question are your next FAQs, in your clients' exact words.

**2. A post-purchase survey that asks only happy clients for a review.** Create a form with the **Feedback survey** template, **Slides** layout, and trim it to two questions:

- From 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us? (slider with points: 0 to 6 is worth 0, 7 to 8 is worth 1, 9 to 10 is worth 2)
- What helped you the most? (short text)

In **Logic → Scoring**, turn on scoring, and in **Logic → Outcomes**, create two ranges. For the 2-point range, set the ending to redirect to your review link (Google, G2 or Trustpilot), with the message "Could you help us with a 2-minute review?" For 0 and 1, a thank-you message and an email alert to your team so they call them. Send it to every client 7 days after delivery.
