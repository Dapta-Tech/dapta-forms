A business sells when three things work together: an offer worth what you charge, people who show up asking for it, and a conversation that ends in yes. If one breaks, the other two can't carry it.

This playbook packs 8 methods, one per problem. Don't read it front to back: find your symptom and jump straight to its method. Each one comes with a template you can fill in in 10 minutes.

- **People say "that's expensive":** method 1. **You guessed your price:** method 2.
- **Nobody reaches out:** method 3. **You don't know if your ads pay off:** method 4. **Lots of work, little profit:** method 5.
- **You feel like you're being grilled:** method 6. **"I'll think about it" and they never come back:** method 7. **You don't know how to ask for the yes:** method 8.

**The only rule:** one method, one full week and one number to track. Switching tactics every two days is the fastest way to never know what worked.

## Method 1: the formula to charge more

When people say you're expensive, it's almost never the price: they don't see enough value. Value comes down to four things you can move:

- **Outcome (raise it):** how badly do they want what you promise? Nobody wants "Spanish lessons." They want to nail the interview.
- **Certainty (raise it):** how sure is it that they'll get there? A guarantee, client stories, a small test before the big commitment.
- **Time (cut it):** how long until they see the result? Design a quick win in the first 24 hours or the first week.
- **Effort (cut it):** how hard is it for them? Templates, done-for-you setup, 10 minutes a day from their phone.

Illustrative example: "Spanish lessons at $15 an hour" competes on price. "Your first job interview in Spanish in 30 days or your money back, 10 minutes a day from your phone" competes on outcome, and you can charge several times more.

<!-- anim: manual-del-vendedor/cuatro-palancas-del-valor | Four sliders raise outcome and certainty and lower time and effort, and the $15-an-hour Spanish lessons offer gets rewritten until it competes on outcome -->

**Template, 10 minutes.** Rewrite your offer by filling in four lines:

```
Real outcome:        [what they actually want, not what you sell]
How I guarantee it:  [guarantee, client story or small test]
Quick win:           [what they get in 24 hours or in the first week]
I do it for them:    [what you take off their plate]
```

## Method 2: find your real price

Nobody knows their right price on day one. Instead of guessing, raise it in steps and let buyers tell you where to stop.

1. **10 to 20 cheap clients.** Charge little so you learn fast and build results to show.
2. **+20% every 5 clients.** Don't announce it: the next client just hears the new price.
3. **For every 10 conversations, count** how much money comes in: price × how many said yes. When that money drops, go back one step.

Illustrative example from a web designer: at $1,500, 5 out of 10 say yes ($7,500); at $1,800, 5 out of 10 ($9,000); at $2,200, 3 out of 10 ($6,600). Their price is $1,800. Wait until you have 10 conversations per step: with 2 or 3, a single "no" looks like a trend.

## Method 3: the 4 ways to get clients

If nobody buys from you, it's almost always because nobody knows you exist. Cross two questions: are you talking to one person or to many? Do they know you, or are they strangers? You get four boxes, and that's all there is.

| | They know you | Strangers |
|---|---|---|
| **One to one** | Warm: past clients, friends, contacts. This week: message someone who bought from you 6 months ago with an offer that expires Friday. | Cold: messages and calls to people who don't know you. This week: 20 businesses that could buy from you, one personal message to each. |
| **One to many** | Content: posts, videos, a newsletter. This week: answer the question you get asked most in one video. | Ads: you pay and tomorrow thousands see you. If you don't know yet which message sells, paying just makes you lose money faster. |

Pick one box and give it the first hours of your day. Track conversations started per week, not likes. A little of everything ends in a month of not knowing what worked.

## Method 4: how much to pay for a client

Is a $20 lead expensive? It depends on what that client brings in. Run this math:

```
A. Pays you per month - cost to serve them  = ______
B. Months they stay                         = ______
What you make = A × B                       = ______
C. Cost per lead × leads for 1 sale         = ______
Ratio = what you make ÷ C                   = ______
```

Illustrative example from a yoga studio: ($80 - $30) × 6 months = $300 each client brings in. Getting one costs $20 × 5 leads = $100. A 3 to 1 ratio: at 3 or more, you can spend more on getting clients. If it's lower, raise your price (method 2), get them to stay longer, sell them something else or close more of the people already coming in (part 3).

## Method 5: sell to your best clients

Almost always, a small group of clients brings in most of your money, and another group pays little and eats up your time. Keep the good ones and go find more like them.

1. **Sort by money.** Your clients from the last year, from most to least profit. Mark the top 20%.
2. **Write down what they share.** Who they are, where they come from, how they found you, what they bought first.
3. **Repeat with the bottom ones.** Whatever they have in common is exactly what you should stop chasing.

## Method 6: how to answer any question

Whoever asks the questions runs the sale. If you rush to answer, the client becomes the judge. Before you reply, follow three steps: **repeat** their question in your own words, **praise** it ("that's exactly what my best clients ask") and **ask** something about their question.

| If they ask... | Answer with... |
|---|---|
| How much does it cost? | What range did you have in mind to solve this? |
| How much experience do you have? | What are you worried could go wrong? |
| Why you and not someone else? | What didn't you like about what you tried before? |
| How long does it take? | When do you need it, and what happens if it's not ready? |
| Can you send me some info? | Sure. Which part do you want to look at closely, so I send you exactly that? |
| Do you offer a discount? | What would it need to include for the price to make sense to you? |

Never ask "do you have any questions?" You're handing them the wheel. Better: "which part would you like to dig into?"

## Method 7: how to answer any excuse

"I don't have time" is almost never the real reason. An excuse is an onion with three layers (circumstances, other people, themselves), and the reason sits in the center. Don't argue with the outer layer: peel it.

<!-- anim: manual-del-vendedor/cebolla-de-la-excusa | A three-layer onion with the excuse "I don't have time" gets peeled layer by layer: circumstances, other people and themselves, until it reaches the real reason -->

| Excuse | Answer |
|---|---|
| I don't have time | When would be a better time? If the answer is never, isn't that exactly why you need this? |
| I don't have the money | You don't have it, or you don't see it being worth it? If it were free, would you start today? |
| I need to talk to my partner | What will they ask? Let's solve it now so you go in with answers. |
| My boss has to approve it | What's the first thing they'll look at? Want me to help you build the case? |
| I'll think about it | On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you? What's missing to get to 10? |

## Method 8: how to close a sale in 4 steps

Every client walks in guarding their wallet. When you tell them they don't need something, they lower their guard, and whatever you say next sounds like advice.

1. **What they don't need:** "Your brakes are fine." Taking things off the list buys trust.
2. **The one thing they do need:** one recommendation, like a doctor. Not a menu of ten options.
3. **Two options:** not "do you want it?" but "Monday or Tuesday?"
4. **Easy payment:** a link right then, zero extra steps.

```
You don't need...    [what you're taking off]
What you do need     [your recommendation]
Because...           [the reason in one sentence]
Do you prefer A or B? [two options that are already a yes]
You can pay with...  [how they pay]
```

If you can't fill in this script, go back to method 6: you still need more questions.

## Do it with dForms

Methods 2, 6 and 8 depend on walking into the call knowing three things: how much they can invest, when they need it and what worries them. Instead of finding out on the call, ask beforehand with a 2-minute form.

**The form: "Before your call"** (built from the Lead qualifier template).

<!-- img: manual-del-vendedor/form-antes-de-tu-llamada | "Before your call" form in dForms with the budget range question and its points, next to the three outcomes: cold, warm and hot -->

1. In dForms go to **Forms → Create form**, name it "Before your call," pick the **Slides** layout and start from the **Lead qualifier** template. Swap its questions for these:
   - What do you want to solve? (single choice with your 3 services, no points)
   - What budget range do you have in mind for this? (single choice: your low range +1, your middle range +3, your high range +5)
   - When do you need it? (This month +5, In 1 to 3 months +3, Just looking 0)
   - What are you worried could go wrong? (long text, no points: it's your opener from method 6)
   - Name, email and phone, at the end.
2. In **Logic → Scoring**, turn on scoring. Your max lands at 10.
3. In **Logic → Outcomes**, create three ranges: 0 to 3 "Cold" (you send them your free content), 4 to 7 "Warm" (you message them this week with a single option), 8 or more "Hot."
4. Add a **Scheduler** question after the urgency one, connect Calendly and in **Edit logic** set it to "Show when Score so far is Greater than 7." Only hot leads see your calendar.
5. Turn on the **Partial submit point** after the email question, so you don't lose people who drop off halfway.
6. In **Connect → Integrations**, send every response to HubSpot with its score and outcome, or to a webhook. Publish it and put the link in your bio and in your auto-reply message.

Before every call, open their response: you already know their range (method 2), their worry (method 6) and what to recommend (method 8).
